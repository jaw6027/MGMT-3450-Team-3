## Why test TikTok?

Short vertical videos can make one load-review idea concrete with a worked example, a whiteboard or a founder explanation. Comment replies offer a way to learn what dispatchers actually need. TikTok is a channel hypothesis, not proven audience fit: evaluate qualified conversations and interest alongside reach. No audience size, growth rate or industry statistic is assumed here.

## Audience and positioning

Primary: independent truck dispatchers who review offers, coordinate schedules and talk with brokers. Secondary: owner-operators who make their own load decisions. Aim for practical, plain-language explanations useful to a new dispatcher without talking down to experienced people. Angel's approximately eight years of trucking industry experience is user-provided context, not an independently verified credential. Ask him to confirm his wording and any personal anecdotes before filming. Do not write invented customer stories or quotes in his voice.

Lead with “AI should be an assistant to dispatchers, not a replacement.” State the problem, show the inputs, and leave the decision with the person. Describe future features with “proposed,” “intended” or “in development.” A 1–100 score is a proposed summary, not a validated measure, acceptance rule, earnings prediction or safety assessment.

## Publishing and production rhythm

The suggested launch window is **November 9–December 8, 2026**, with **13 posts** on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays (plus the final Monday). The 30 daily entries include 17 preparation, response and review days, each with a reserve video concept. These reserve concepts are not extra daily publishing commitments. Shift the dates together if launch moves. Start by testing 6 p.m. in the audience's primary time zone, then compare actual audience activity; this is a starting experiment, not a claimed optimal time. The tracker stores calendar dates only; time and time zone belong in notes.

Batch record three short videos each week. Allow a separate review pass for captions, arithmetic, claims and readable overlays. Shoot vertical 9:16, keep the speaker clearly audible, put text away from TikTok controls, and manually check captions. Use Barlow Condensed headings and Barlow body text when available, with the approved brand fallbacks. Use charcoal on off-white or amber and off-white on charcoal. Do not use small amber text on off-white. Existing brand assets are the only logos.

## Engagement and awareness

Open with a specific question or decision, then teach one idea. Ask for a real workflow insight rather than generic engagement. Reply to relevant comments within the next working day when possible; turn recurring questions into response videos and credit commenters only with permission. Use three to five relevant hashtags from the calendar; avoid unrelated trending tags. Review completion and meaningful replies to compare formats. Repeat the mission and visual identity consistently. Use Angel on camera and practical examples, not hype or fabricated endorsements.

Share approved posts with the website and other social leads for consistency, without editing their folders. Collaborate with relevant trucking educators only after agreeing on factual claims and any required sponsorship disclosure. Use original or properly licensed audio suitable for the chosen TikTok account type. Never film while driving. Use fictional loads and remove customer, broker, rate confirmation, plate, phone and route-identifying information from visuals.

## From awareness to a trial

1. Teach a useful concept without requiring a signup.
2. Explain the proposed workflow with an unmistakable “Concept mockup · in development” label that stays on screen.
3. Invite questions about the inputs and limitations.
4. When Angel has approved a real destination, offer a transparent update or demo request, with clear privacy information.
5. Only when access works, invite viewers to try it. Ask which step helped and where human judgment was still needed. Never guarantee better rates, savings or profitability.

## Angel's measurement routine

Review weekly, using the same observation window (for example, seven days after each post). Enter cumulative TikTok counts with an “as of” date in notes; do not mix daily increments with lifetime totals. Compare a post's first seven days with other posts' first seven days rather than comparing a one-day post to a month-old one.

| Metric | What it helps answer | Where to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Views | Did this topic reach viewers? | Tracker |
| Likes, comments, shares | Did viewers respond or pass it on? | Tracker |
| Engagement by views | How much visible interaction per view? | Calculated in tracker |
| Watch time, completion, retention | Where do viewers drop off? | TikTok analytics; summarize in notes |
| Saves and qualified questions | Was this practically useful? | Analytics/comments; notes |
| Profile visits and follows | Did interest carry beyond one video? | Analytics; weekly notes |
| Approved link visits, demo requests, activated trials | Did interest become intent and use? | Approved web/product analytics; notes |

**Formula:** `(likes + comments + shares) / views × 100`. Require all four nonnegative integer counts and views greater than zero. Blank means unknown; zero means a measured zero. The overview uses pooled interactions divided by pooled views for Published posts with complete counts; it is not an average of individual percentages. Total views includes Published posts with a measured view count, including those with incomplete engagement counts. Interactions are events, not distinct people, so engagement can exceed 100%. This definition excludes saves and is not claimed to be TikTok's official rate.

Set a baseline from the first month rather than inventing growth targets. Choose the next experiment from watch-through, qualified comments and tracked interest. More views alone do not establish more customers. Record one concrete next step after each weekly review.
