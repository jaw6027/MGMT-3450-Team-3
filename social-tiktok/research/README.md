## Keep future entries comparable

Use the next unused entry number and one file per reviewed video, even when reviewing the same account again. Add a row to this index rather than making revised copies of prior entries. Add clarifications within the original entry with a dated note and retain the original observation and its source.

Use these fields for each future entry:

1. **Identity and provenance:** entry number, account, who recommended it, exact video URL or ID, video publication date if visible, observation date/time/time zone, and who supplied the notes. Mark missing items as not supplied; do not infer them.
2. **Video observation:** subject, format, opening hook if recorded, duration if recorded, and load details. Distinguish approximate numbers from exact numbers and spoken facts from the reviewer's interpretation.
3. **Metric snapshot:** displayed views, likes, comments and shares only when observed, with the observation time. Keep displayed totals separate from the number of comments actually reviewed. Leave unknown metrics unknown.
4. **Comment evidence:** reference label (for example C1), verbatim quote only if captured accurately, otherwise a labeled paraphrase; theme, apparent viewpoint, uncertainty and any contradictory responses. Do not invent commenter identities, agreement counts or ordering.
5. **Interpretation:** the question suggested by each observation, evidence limits and what would strengthen or challenge the interpretation. One viewer's description of a job or business practice is not a verified definition.
6. **Content opportunities:** suggested topic, educational point, discussion question, relevant existing calendar day/script, and any factual or owner review needed. Keep these as candidates until explicitly adopted.
7. **Product boundary:** source of owner-provided feature information, whether it is proposed or confirmed, and what must not be claimed. Do not infer working features, scoring weights or validation from a topic's popularity.
8. **Next comparison and decision status:** questions for subsequent videos/accounts; whether an idea is only logged, proposed, approved or applied. Research collection does not change a post's publication status.

## How to use the log

Compare whether a theme reappears across different videos and accounts, and record disagreements as well as agreement. Recurrence can help prioritize another content experiment, but a convenience sample still does not measure how the whole industry thinks. Look for dispatchers' information needs rather than copying a creator's exact content or treating their viewers as proven ProfitLane customers.

Use original fictional examples when developing future videos. Do not reuse footage, identifiable comments, voices or customer material without appropriate permission. Keep commenter identities out of these notes unless they are needed and permission is established.
