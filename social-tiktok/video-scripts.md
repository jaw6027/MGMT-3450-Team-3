# First ten TikTok videos · creative filming scripts

## Start here

These are complete draft scripts with role-labeled dialogue and timed storyboards. **SAY** contains the words to record; read only your role's lines. Camera, action, edit, sound and overlay instructions are not spoken. A helper can film and play multiple roles in separate takes. Timecodes are elapsed minutes:seconds and are planned edit timings, not measured footage. Rehearse at a natural pace and adjust boundaries without rushing; countdowns and reveals include short visual holds.

Angel speaks in videos **1, 4, 6 and 10**, including a founder conversation in 4. These are draft lines for his approval, grounded in his stated vision, not verbatim historical quotes or invented personal experiences. He is a client-to-client speaker, not an influencer character. Videos **2 and 7** are fictional dispatcher–broker role-plays. Videos **3, 6 and 8** use viewer challenges or comparisons. Video **5** is a hands-on fuel demonstration. Video **9** is a clearly labeled paper concept walkthrough. Categories intentionally overlap; each has a different visual engine.

## Small-business production kit

A phone, inexpensive stand or stack of books, window light, quiet desk, paper, marker, calculator and a helper are enough. A toy truck can be a folded paper arrow. Draw schematic routes rather than sourcing map footage. Use a basic phone editor for split screens, freeze frames and simple counters; each storyboard also works with physical paper reveals and straight cuts. No premium animation, second camera or working product screen is required. Film one angle, move the phone, then record the insert. Get a clean take of each dialogue side before assembling the scene.

Use the existing brand assets unchanged: charcoal #2B2F33, amber #F5A623, off-white #F7F5F0 and approved white artwork. Use Barlow Condensed for headings and Barlow for body text, with brand fallbacks. Put charcoal text on off-white/amber; use off-white/amber on charcoal. Avoid small amber text on off-white. Keep logos proportionate and overlays inside TikTok's safe area. Subtitles follow the dialogue; overlays highlight the idea and should appear only at the named reveal.

Shoot stationary in vertical 9:16. No filming while driving. Original footage of a stationary toy/desk is enough; any real trucking B-roll must be safe, original or properly licensed and contain no identifying customer data. Use clean voice audio and quiet licensed music only where it does not hide speech. No strobe effects or copied competitor footage.

Keep **FICTIONAL ROLE-PLAY / HYPOTHETICAL INPUTS** labels throughout the applicable video, and **CONCEPT / IN DEVELOPMENT** labels throughout concept demonstrations. Any score is an arbitrary illustration, never validated or a profitability guarantee. Do not create plausible-looking real product screens, AI scans, company flags, customer testimonials or successful booking outcomes. Independent dispatchers remain primary; independent operators starting their own dispatching are secondary. Product exploration/demo CTAs wait for an approved real route. The four research entries and exact stakeholder responses remain unchanged.

## 2. The miles before the load

**Draft slot:** Day 3 · 2026-11-11

**Target length:** About 78 seconds · 147 spoken words plus visual holds

**Creative concept:** A tempting loaded-mile figure gets interrupted by the road to pickup.

**Purpose:** Make total-mile arithmetic part of a believable load-review conversation.

**Who appears / roles:** Two helpers play Dispatcher and Broker; one person can play both using separate phone positions and labeled jump cuts. Angel does not appear.

**Props / materials:** Two phone/headset setups, fictional offer sheet, toy truck or paper arrow, hand-drawn route, calculator.

**Low-budget setup:** Give Dispatcher the left side of the frame and Broker the right. Keep role labels and FICTIONAL ROLE-PLAY visible. Route is a schematic, not a real geographic trip.

**Opening hook:** Five hundred miles. From where?

### 00:00–00:03

**SAY**

> **Broker:** Five hundred miles.

> **Dispatcher:** From where?

**SHOW / ACTION:** Fictional offer sheet lands on Dispatcher’s desk; they lift one eyebrow and reach for the phone.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Fast close-up of the sheet, then Dispatcher’s face.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Hard cut with a phone-pickup movement; role labels appear immediately.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Fictional role-play

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Soft phone pickup sound, not a copied real call.

### 00:03–00:15

**SAY**

> **Broker:** The offer is twelve hundred dollars for five hundred loaded miles.

> **Dispatcher:** Let me check the miles to pickup before I compare that rate.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Split-screen Broker right and Dispatcher left. Dispatcher circles loaded on the paper sheet.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Alternating over-shoulder phone angles; insert on the circled word.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Freeze the call for a fraction of a second when loaded is circled.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Fictional offer: $1,200 / 500 loaded miles

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Record each side cleanly; do not use radio-style distortion that hides words.

### 00:15–00:28

**SAY**

> **Dispatcher:** In this example, the truck is one hundred empty miles from pickup. Watch what happens if I leave that drive out of the comparison.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Move a toy truck along the empty leg before it reaches the pickup pin.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Overhead route strip; only the helper’s hand and toy are visible.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Use an amber moving dashed line for the empty leg; a counter runs 0 to 100.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** 100 deadhead miles before pickup

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Short wheel-slide sound; narration replaces the phone-call audio.

### 00:28–00:41

**SAY**

> **Dispatcher:** Twelve hundred divided by five hundred is two forty per loaded mile. But one hundred empty plus five hundred loaded makes six hundred total miles.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Calculator shows the first division; then empty and loaded strips join end to end.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Macro calculator insert, then overhead on the route join.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Match cut on the plus sign to the joined route; hold 100 + 500 = 600.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** $2.40/loaded mile → 600 total miles

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** A single calculator click is enough; leave two seconds for the equation.

### 00:41–00:54

**SAY**

> **Dispatcher:** Now twelve hundred divided by six hundred is two dollars per total mile. Same offer, different comparison. Neither number is profit or an automatic yes.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Two division results sit side by side, labeled loaded and total; no acceptance stamp.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Top-down with results large enough for a phone screen.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Reveal the total-mile result from under a flap; underline revenue, not profit.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** $1,200 ÷ 600 = $2.00/total mile

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Keep speech steady and let the reveal hold.

### 00:54–01:07

**SAY**

> **Dispatcher:** Back to the call. I also need the schedule, requirements and costs before deciding.

> **Broker:** Which detail do you want to confirm first?

**SHOW / ACTION:** Return to the split-screen; Dispatcher points to blank schedule and requirement fields.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Same opposing phone angles, with a tight insert on the blanks.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Use a quick rewind of the toy to signal returning to the conversation, not a second trip.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** What is still missing?

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Resume the original room tone and phone-side dialogue.

### 01:07–01:18

**SAY**

> **Dispatcher:** Your turn. What would you ask next? Start with a question, not just a yes or no. This is a fictional teaching example.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Dispatcher looks away from the phone to the viewer; the route remains visible beside them.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Medium close-up with the route at the lower edge.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Hold the question for two seconds; end on the original pickup pin for a visual loop.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Comment your next question

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** No celebratory sound or claimed booking outcome.

**Caption:** Fictional offer: $1,200, 500 loaded miles… plus the miles before pickup. What would you ask next?

**Hashtags:** #ProfitLane #TruckDispatch #IndependentDispatcher #LoadPlanning

**Natural CTA / production condition:** Comment with the first detail you would confirm before discussing the rate.

## 3. Revenue is not take-home pay

**Draft slot:** Day 5 · 2026-11-13

**Target length:** About 82 seconds · 155 spoken words plus visual holds

**Creative concept:** The viewer guesses what remains as expense cards slide into the payment.

**Purpose:** Distinguish payment, a partial remainder and actual take-home pay.

**Who appears / roles:** Helper’s hands and a conversational helper voiceover. Angel does not appear.

**Props / materials:** Paper $1,200 payment card, removable $400 fuel / $50 toll / $300 allocated-cost cards, folded question flap, calculator.

**Low-budget setup:** Film overhead on off-white paper. Build each calculation in camera; use a phone editor only for counters and a two-second countdown.

**Opening hook:** Twelve hundred. How much stays?

### 00:00–00:03

**SAY**

> **Host:** Twelve hundred. How much stays?

**SHOW / ACTION:** Hands cover a folded remainder flap under a $1,200 payment card.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Overhead close-up; no presenter introduction.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Quick push-in made by moving the phone slightly closer in a second take.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Fictional expense challenge

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Paper tap marks the hook.

### 00:03–00:15

**SAY**

> **Host:** Before you guess, these are made-up teaching numbers. They are not typical industry costs or a ProfitLane calculation. Keep your answer in mind.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Set four face-down expense cards around the payment; leave the answer flap closed.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Locked top-down, with all numbers inside the central safe area.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** A two-second 3–2–1 graphic invites a guess; do not claim a live poll.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Hypothetical inputs • Not a product result

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Brief ticking under the visual hold only.

### 00:15–00:28

**SAY**

> **Host:** First, suppose fuel is four hundred dollars. Then tolls are fifty. Slide those out of the payment. What is left at this step?

**SHOW / ACTION:** Flip fuel and toll cards and slide each under the payment while a handwritten counter drops.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Close-up on each flip, then return to the overhead master shot.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Reveal $750 only after the question; use charcoal numbers on off-white.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** $1,200 − $400 − $50 = ?

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Two restrained taps match the two deductions.

### 00:28–00:42

**SAY**

> **Host:** Seven hundred and fifty. Now suppose we allocate three hundred in other operating costs. Here comes the next reveal: four hundred and fifty remains in this example.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Move the $300 card, then lift the remainder flap to reveal $450.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Side close-up of the lifting flap, then full worksheet overhead.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** One clean answer reveal; no green profit badge or cash-rain animation.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** $750 − $300 = $450 remaining

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Use a quiet page-flip sound rather than a jackpot cue.

### 00:42–00:56

**SAY**

> **Host:** Did you call that profit? Wait. It is a remainder after only the costs we listed. It is not automatically a wage or final take-home pay.

**SHOW / ACTION:** A WAIT tab slides over the word profit on a separate question card, leaving the calculation visible.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Overhead, then a tight insert on costs listed versus costs not listed.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Widen the frame to reveal space below the worksheet for more questions.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** A partial remainder is not final take-home pay

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Give wait a short pause; keep the delivery friendly, not a gotcha.

### 00:56–01:10

**SAY**

> **Host:** Labor, maintenance, insurance, financing, applicable fees and taxes may still need review. Check what applies to your business and avoid counting the same expense twice.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Fan out possible-cost cards, keeping them unpriced; do not pretend the list is complete.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Low desk-level fan reveal, then return to the full overhead shot.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Bring in three cards at a time to avoid a wall of text.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Which applicable costs are still missing?

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** Narration stays clear; no added tax rates or percentages.

### 01:10–01:22

**SAY**

> **Host:** What cost would you check next? Put one in the comments. A useful review starts with what the payment needs to cover, not the headline alone.

**SHOW / ACTION:** Leave the empty next-cost slot in frame and place a pencil beside it.

**CAMERA / ANGLE:** Locked top-down; optional hand points to the blank slot.

**TRANSITION / GRAPHICS:** Hold the question two seconds, then fold the answer flap shut to loop.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** What would you check next?

**SOUND / DELIVERY:** End on the paper fold with no sales outro.

**Caption:** The payment card says $1,200. What has it still got to cover? All numbers are fictional teaching inputs.

**Hashtags:** #ProfitLane #TruckDispatch #IndependentDispatcher #LoadPlanning
